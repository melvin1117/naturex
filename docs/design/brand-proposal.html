A name that carries
the argument.

NatureX argues one thing: a living wetland should be worth more than the factory that would replace it, and it proves that with ratings anyone can check. This proposal starts from that argument and from how the team got here. It offers five directions, each a name that can carry the argument on its own and a logo drawn from a real instrument or record, not decoration. It ends with the full system every direction shares: icon pack, typography, colour, motion and voice.

v1 retiredVersion 1 (Kept, Quadrat, Lichen, Wildgrade) offered pleasant words with no link to the problem. None of them told a judge why nature is undervalued or what this product fixes. They are listed under set aside with the reasons.

01 · How we got here

The story the name has to tell

Every claim below is from the project's own docs or a cited source (listed at the end).

The spark

A wetland near Mexico City, hemmed in by factories

A documentary showed a community's wetland under industrial pressure and claimed wetlands may be worth $25,000–50,000 per acre per year in services nobody pays for. That case became the hero site and the opening of the video.

Source: pm-spec §6 and dev-spec §7.3. Team: add the documentary's title here. Our engine is more cautious: pure inland wetland comes to $34,018/ha/yr, and the hero site $12,915/ha/yr. We report that gap rather than tune it away.

The insight

“The bees never sent an invoice.”

Markets only see dead nature: timber, minerals, land for factories. A community protecting a wetland gets one tempting offer: sell once.

Source: one-pager.

Sep 2023

Wall Street tried to list nature

The NYSE filed a rule to list Natural Asset Companies, built with Intrinsic Exchange Group. Every listed company would have had to publish an Ecological Performance Report, the same report this product generates.

Sources: Federal Register, Oct 2023; IEG framework (SEC exhibit).

Jan 2024

…and pulled back

The NYSE withdrew the rule after opposition from 25 state attorneys general and others. The concerns were about who controls the land and whether the values could be trusted.

Source: The Corporate Counsel, Jan 2024.

The gap

The missing piece was never the exchange. It was the rating.

“The bottleneck is cheap, credible, continuous measurement” (pm-spec §1). It also has to belong to the community, and anyone has to be able to check it.

What we built

An AI rating agency for living land

Nemotron agents read five years of satellite record and the local news, “and what each says about the other”. Tested formulas set the grade. The Auditor may move it by at most ±10, and only with cited evidence. Every rating is sealed in a hash-chained ledger. The community keeps its land; supporters back it with play money.

Source: Devpost draft, Oct 22.

The hero site

Xochimilco: the place that already wrote the argument into law

It is the last remnant of the lakes of the Valley of Mexico. It is the only wild home of the axolotl, a critically endangered salamander that regrows its own limbs. And it lies in Mexico City, whose 2017 constitution (Article 13) says: “Nature has the right to exist, persist, maintain and regenerate its vital cycles.” Aldo Leopold defined exactly what the Health score measures: “Health is the capacity of the land for self-renewal.”

Sources: Lake Xochimilco; Mexico City Constitution, Art. 13; Leopold, A Sand County Almanac (1949).

02 · The brief

Five tests every name must pass

1. Carries the argumentWithout explanation, it hints that living nature has a value the market misses.
2. Sounds like a ratingA verdict you trust, not a token you trade. “Rating agency first, exchange second.”
3. Belongs to Rosa tooThe community is the protagonist. The name can't read as Wall Street's.
4. Survives a downgradeSay it in a sober sentence: “___ lowered Xochimilco to BBB.” Cute names break here.
5. OwnableNo collision in climate, finance or ratings; no crypto echo.

03 · Direction A · recommended

Standing

One English word that means the land is alive, that it is creditworthy, and that it has a voice, all at once.

Recommended

Tagline

Worth more standing.

“In 1972 a law professor asked whether trees should have standing. In 2017 Mexico City wrote it into its constitution. We built the missing piece: a rating that shows what that standing is worth.”

The 15-second opener for the video and the Devpost page.

Ecology

Standing forest, standing water

Timber is priced when it falls; a wetland when it's drained and sold. Standing is the state the market ignores, and the state we rate. A wetland is, literally, standing water.

Finance

In good standing

The plainest credit verdict in English. A grade is a statement of standing. Upgrades and downgrades read naturally: “standing raised”, “standing lowered”.

Law and rights

Should trees have standing?

Christopher Stone's 1972 essay started the rights-of-nature movement. Standing is the right to be heard. Here, a community's land finally gets a voice that counts in money.

The mark · the staff gauge

  1. The post is the measuring staff hydrologists drive into a wetland to read its water level. It stands in the water.
  2. The E-pattern ticks are the real “Style E” markings: an instrument, not an illustration.
  3. The lime waterline is the reading. In the product it sits at the site's actual score.
  4. Below the line the post fades, as if seen through water.

Said in the product

Xochimilco Wetland · A · in good standing
Standing lowered to BBB: encroachment reported at the northern edge (12 Sep 2026).
Rosa's cooperative was funded while the wetland kept standing.
“Check its standing.” (what a supporter says to a friend)

Living logo · drag to re-rate

76.2A

Strong health · in good standing

0 · D30 · C50 · BB70 · A90 · AAA

Each site's page, favicon and share card can show its own reading. The logo is a measurement, so it moves when the land changes.

Ownable?No conflict found in climate, finance or ratings. It's a common word, so register it as a word mark in financial-information services and use a qualified domain (for example standing.earth). WatchIn the US, “Standing” can echo Standing Rock and its water protectors. That's a respectful resonance, not a conflict. Never borrow it in copy. 04 · Direction B

Living Standard

The institutional direction. The world's biggest rating agency still carries the word Standard; this name sets one for what is alive.

Tagline

The standard for what's alive.

“Every investor knows the word Standard from Standard & Poor's, which began in 1906 as the Standard Statistics Bureau. For a community near Mexico City, the wetland is its standard of living: the water, the flood protection, the chinampa harvest. We built the rating standard for that.”

Finance

A rating standard

The vocabulary judges already trust. Standard Statistics (1906) became half of S&P. A rating agency's job is to be the standard others compare against.

People

Standard of living

The services we value (water supply, flood control, food) are literally the community's standard of living. Destroying the wetland lowers it; that's the argument, in two words.

Horticulture

A “standard” is a tree

Gardeners call a tree trained on a single upright stem with a round crown a standard. That's the mark: a stem you can measure, a crown that shows the grade.

The mark · the standard tree

  1. The stem is a measuring rule, ticked like a survey staff.
  2. The crown is a dial. The lime arc is the site's health score, so the tree fills out as the land heals.
  3. The ground line: the rating starts from the land itself.

The crown reads the grade

Xochimilco Wetland meets the A standard.
Living Standard lowered Xochimilco to BBB: encroachment at the northern edge.
Ownable?No company found under this name in ratings, ESG or climate. But it's a common economic phrase, so search results are crowded and the plain words are hard to register; the mark carries the ownership. WatchIt leans on S&P's vocabulary. Present it as an homage to rating culture, never as an affiliation.

05 · Direction C

Natural Interest

The economics direction. It puts Rosa's choice, sell once or keep it alive, into the one financial idea everyone understands.

Tagline

Live on the interest.

“Economists call clean water, flood protection and carbon storage the interest that nature's capital pays. Draining a wetland for a factory spends the principal: one payment, gone forever. We rate the interest, so a community can live on it.”

Economics

Interest, not principal

Natural capital is a stock; ecosystem services are the flow it yields (Costanza; Daily; Helm). “Sell once” spends the stock. A rating of the yearly value measures the interest.

Ownership

Having an interest

Supporters take an interest in a site: a stake, with play money. The community keeps its interest in its own land: it never sells.

Attention

Worth our interest

Nature only gets attention when it's being destroyed. A live rating gives a living place the market's interest while it's still alive.

The mark · the percent sign

  1. A percent sign, the symbol of interest, rebuilt from nature.
  2. The leaf is natural capital: the stock that must stay.
  3. The drop is what it yields: water, the first service in every wetland's report.
  4. The lime sprout on the stem is the interest accruing. It unfurls when a quarter's rating lands.

Said in the product

Xochimilco Wetland pays $2.1M–$2.7M a year in natural interest · rated A.
Natural Interest lowered Xochimilco to BBB: the encroachment cut the yearly value it pays.
Sell the wetland once, or live on its interest every year.
Ownable?No company found under this name in finance or climate. Watch“Interest” can read as an investment return, which the project's sandbox rules forbid. Copy must always say the interest is what the land pays to people (its services), never a return to supporters. If that's too fine a line for the demo, choose another direction.

06 · Direction D

Witness Tree

The evidence-first direction. The name says the product doesn't just score land; it keeps a record that can be proved later.

Tagline

Every site has a witness.

“For centuries, surveyors marked a living tree at each land corner so the boundary could be proved later. They called it a witness tree. We give every site two witnesses, the satellite record and the local news, and an Auditor that listens to both.”

Surveying

A tree that proves a boundary

Surveyors blazed a bearing tree near each corner marker. If the marker was lost, the tree proved where the line was. Listing a site starts with drawing its boundary.

Evidence

Two witnesses

The product reads the satellite record and the local news “and what each says about the other”. The Auditor cross-examines both. That's testimony, not a vibe.

Time

Rings remember

A tree's rings record every year it stood. Five rings in the mark: the five years of satellite record behind each rating.

The mark · the blazed rings

  1. Five rings: the five years of satellite record.
  2. The blaze: the surveyor's mark, cut into the outer ring. Lime, because it is this quarter's reading.
  3. The rings are slightly irregular: grown, not drawn with a compass.

Said in the product

Witness Tree rates Xochimilco Wetland A.
Witness Tree lowered Xochimilco to BBB, citing satellite loss at the northern edge and two local news reports.
“What did the witnesses see?” opens the evidence timeline.
Ownable?Small existing users: “My Witness Tree” (a faith app) and Witness Tree Media (photo and video for environmental groups). Workable, but less clean than Standing. WatchTwo words, and “witness” can feel legal or grim. Less warm for Rosa.

07 · Direction E · lowest cost

NatureX, with a reason for the X

Keep the name and stop the X from meaning “crypto”. Give it the product's real job instead.

Tagline

Where the evidence crosses.

“The X in NatureX is where two records cross: what the satellites saw and what the local news reported. Where they agree, the rating holds. Where they don't, the Auditor asks why.”

The product

Two lines, one point

One stroke is the five-year greenness curve; the other is the dated news line with its event ticks. The rating lives where they meet.

Surveying

Triangulation

A position is fixed where two independent sight lines cross. One source is a claim; two crossing sources are a fix.

Cost

Nothing to rename

Devpost, repo, video and ~72 strings stay. Only the wordmark, favicon and one story line change.

Ownable?No. Naturex is a botanical-extracts company owned by Givaudan, in an adjacent space. Fine for a hackathon; a problem for anything after it. WatchSome people will still read “-X” as crypto or hype. The story has to be told every time.

11 · Icon pack

One family, one accent

Every product feature gets an icon from the same family, so they read as one product next to the Lucide icons the app already uses. Each card names the Lucide icon to use if the team prefers to ship standard icons first.

Grid and stroke. 24 px grid, 1.75 px stroke, round caps and joins. Identical to Lucide's metrics.
One accent. Each icon lights exactly one part: what the feature confirms or points at. Filled lime keeps an ink outline, so it passes contrast on light surfaces.
Two surfaces. Lime-ink accent strokes on light; lime on the dark machine layer (trace, tally, agents).

Sizes

Legible from 16 px. Below 20 px, drop interior detail rather than thin the stroke.

Rules

  • Icons always come with a text label in navigation; icon-only buttons need an accessible name.
  • Never recolour the accent to signal state; state lives in text and the grade colour.
  • Agent icons appear only in the trace and tour hints, never as decoration.
  • Ship as an SVG sprite or React components under components/icons/, with the same props as Lucide.

13 · Colour

Same palette, one job per colour

The palette stays. What makes it ownable is giving each colour one job, so colour carries information instead of decoration. The health ramp, the same green-to-clay scale satellites use for vegetation, becomes the signature.

Rules

  • R1 Lime means “the reading” or “confirmed”. Remove it from icon tiles and decorative chips.
  • R2 Dark surfaces are only for the machine layer, so dark always means “the AI is showing its work”.
  • R3 Clay always comes with ▼ and words. Never alarm red: this is a rating agency, not a siren.
  • R4 Drop lilac: it has no job, and a fifth accent dilutes the ramp.
  • R5 Grade badges use dark text on BBB, BB and B.

Contrast issues found in the app

  • “Not trading yet” badge: white on clay is 3.3:1, below the 4.5:1 minimum.
  • BBB/BB with white text is about 2.5–3:1 (fixed by R5).
  • White text over satellite photos depends on the image; always keep the dark scrim behind it.

The logo moment, per direction

Each mark has one meaningful animation, played in the product exactly once per journey (when a rating lands), and in the video's last frame.
