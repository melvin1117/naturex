01

The story

Every claim below is from the project's own docs or a cited source. Tell it in this order: the place, the problem, the failed attempt, the missing piece, and what we built.

The spark

A wetland near Mexico City, hemmed in by factories

A documentary showed a community's wetland under industrial pressure and claimed wetlands may be worth tens of thousands of dollars per acre per year in services nobody pays for. That case became the hero site and the opening of the video.

Source: pm-spec §6, dev-spec §7.3. Our engine is more cautious than the film, and says so.

The insight

“The bees never sent an invoice.”

Markets only see nature once it's dead: timber, minerals, land for factories. A community protecting a wetland gets one tempting offer: sell once.

Source: one-pager.

Sep 2023

Wall Street tried to list nature

The NYSE filed a rule to list Natural Asset Companies. Each would have published an Ecological Performance Report, the same report this product generates.

Source: Federal Register, Oct 2023.

Jan 2024

…and pulled back

The rule was withdrawn after opposition about who controls the land and whether the values could be trusted.

Source: The Corporate Counsel, Jan 2024.

The gap

The missing piece was the rating

“The bottleneck is cheap, credible, continuous measurement” (pm-spec §1). It also has to belong to the community, and anyone has to be able to check it.

What we built

A rating agency for living land

Nemotron agents read five years of satellite record and the local news. Tested formulas set the grade. The Auditor may move it by at most ±10, only with cited evidence. Every rating is sealed into a hash-chained record. The community keeps its land; supporters back it with play money.

The name

WildWorth: wild land has worth

The name is the argument in two words. It has a quiet second layer: in old English place names, -worth meant an enclosed homestead (Tamworth, Kenilworth). A WildWorth site is wild, and it is someone's home.

The hero site

Xochimilco

The last remnant of the lakes of the Valley of Mexico, and the only wild home of the axolotl, a salamander that regrows its own limbs. Aldo Leopold defined what our Health score measures: land health is the land's capacity for self-renewal.

Sources: Lake Xochimilco; Leopold, “The Land Ethic”, A Sand County Almanac (1949).

04

The logo

Two Ws drawn as one line, with the last stroke rising to the land's Health score. It reads as ripples on a wetland, as sedge in the wind and as a value line. The logo is a reading, not a picture.

The line draws, then the reading rises

Anatomy

  1. Two Ws, one line. Wild and Worth, drawn without lifting the pen.
  2. The shape is water, grass and a value line at once. No leaf, no price tag.
  3. The reading is the last stroke. Its height is the site's Health score.
  4. The dot is today's reading, where the line stops.

Construction

  • 100-unit square. Stroke 7.5 units, round caps and joins.
  • The reading's end travels from y 80 (score 0) to y 16 (score 100). The band is shaded.
  • Clear space: one W height (½ the mark) on every side.
  • Mark to wordmark gap: 0.28 × the wordmark's size.

05

Proof of Life

Every quarter, WildWorth issues a Proof of Life for each site: its rating report and seal. The seal is a ring of 100 dots, one per point of health, lit up to the score, with the grade and the quarter in the centre.

On the machine layer
On paper, grade colour
After a downgrade

Use it

  • On the report header, the share card and the steward's home.
  • Always with the site's name and the quarter.
  • “Ecological Performance Report” stays as the subtitle, so finance readers see the lineage.
  • Old seals stay in the Record; a new seal never overwrites one.

Never

  • Abbreviate it to “PoL”: it reads as crypto.
  • Use it as the company name or as a slogan on its own.
  • Pair it with heartbeat lines or a date held up to camera.
  • Issue a seal without its evidence one click away.
06

Colour

The palette from DESIGN.md, with one job per colour. Lime means “the reading”. Dark green means “the AI is showing its work”. Clay means pressure, always with an arrow and words.

Rough proportion on a typical screen. Lime stays rare so it always means something.

The grade ramp

Greens to olive to mustard to clay. A ramp, never a traffic light. A grade always comes with its letter and, on first mention, its score and label.

Direction

Never colour alone. Up and down carry ▲ ▼ and a signed number, or the words “up” and “down”.

Data colours

Land-cover bars and map layers keep Dynamic World's own class colours, so legends match the imagery.

Text pairs, with their contrast

Ratios are calculated on this page from the colour values. WCAG AA needs 4.5:1 for body text and 3:1 for large text and icons.

08

Icons

One family for every WildWorth feature and ecosystem service. Same metrics as Lucide, so they sit next to the generic UI icons (close, menu, chevrons) the app already uses.

Grid and stroke

24 px grid, 2 px padding, 1.75 px stroke, round caps and joins.

One accent

  • Each icon lights exactly one part: what the feature reads or confirms.
  • Filled lime keeps an ink outline, so it passes on light grounds.
  • Accent strokes are lime-ink on light, lime on dark.
  • Never recolour the accent to show state; state lives in text and the grade colour.

Sizes and labels

  • Legible from 16 px. Below 20 px, drop interior detail rather than thin the stroke.
  • Icons in navigation always have a text label; icon-only buttons need an accessible name.
  • Ship as React components under components/icons/ with Lucide's props.

Bea, the field guide

Replaces “Copilot”

Bea

The bee who never sent an invoice, finally speaking up.

Bea answers questions about a site from its own Record: the satellite record, the local news and every rating. She's busy, cheerful and exact. She never overclaims, and she always shows where an answer came from.

  • In the UI: the button is Ask Bea, the panel title is Bea · your field guide, and the Agent Trace shows Bea (Nemotron Nano).
  • How she's drawn: ink only, in the logo's line weight. Her stripe is two carets of the WildWorth zigzag, centred. No colour, by design.
  • How she talks: “Bzz” only to open a first answer. Every number comes from the Record with its source. Never invest, returns or buy.

Open Bea's character sheet

In the repo: docs/design/bea-character.html (states, motion, sizes, product use, voice and don'ts).

The three apps

Motion

Every animation means something. Each plays once, ends on the real value, and is replaced by the final frame when someone prefers reduced motion.

Draw

Something is being measured: the logo line, a boundary, a chart.

900–1100 ms

Rise

A reading arrived: the logo's last stroke, the seal's dots lighting up.

550 ms, after the draw

Count

A number changed: score, value range, balance.

600–900 ms

Stamp

A verdict was issued: the grade reveal and grade change.

520 ms, once

Easing everywhere: cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1), the app's --nx-ease-out. The logo animates only when a rating lands and in the video's last frame, never on hover or as a loader.
