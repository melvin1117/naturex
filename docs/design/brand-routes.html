Worth more wild.

The name for NatureX's product, finalised. WildWorth says the whole argument in two words: wild land has worth. Every quarter it issues a Proof of Life for each site, with the evidence and a date on it.

The idea

What the brand has to say

Belief (the team's line, kept)

Keeping nature alive should pay more than destroying it.

The problem

The market only sees nature once it's sold: timber, minerals, a drained plot for a factory.

What WildWorth says

Living land has worth while it's alive, and here is how much.

Rosa hears

“Our wetland is worth something as it is. We don't have to sell it.”

Arjun hears

“A valuation with a range, a confidence score and a grade I can compare.”

A judge hears

“A rating for nature's value.” In under a second, with no backstory.

The hidden layer

In old English place names, -worth meant an enclosed homestead: Tamworth, Kenilworth, Letchworth. Wildworth reads as a wild place that is also someone's home.

The story for Rosa: the land is wild and it is theirs. Nobody needs to know it for the name to work; it rewards those who notice.

The mark

Two Ws, one line, one reading

The two Ws of WildWorth join into a single line. It reads as ripples on a wetland, as sedge in the wind and as a value line. The last stroke is the reading.

The line draws, then the reading rises

Anatomy

  1. Two Ws, one line: Wild and Worth, drawn without lifting the pen.
  2. The shape is water, grass and a value line at once. No leaf, no price tag.
  3. The last stroke is the reading. Its height is the site's Health score.
  4. The dot is today's value, where the reading stops.

Wordmark

“Wild” is set in the serif, the living side. “Worth” is set in the same light sans as every number in the product, the measured side. The wordmark is nature meeting a number.

Both words start with W, so the mark and the wordmark rhyme without repeating each other.

Same mark, two settings. The mixed setting carries the idea; the all-serif setting is calmer and easier to draw by hand.

Proposed

Mixed. Serif “Wild”, light sans “Worth”. The sans is the product's own numeral face, so the name ends on a number.

Alternative

All serif. One voice, closer to a classic rating agency. Loses the nature-meets-number idea.

Lockups, sizes and site marks

The master mark always shows the hero site's target score (77.6). On a site page, the mark shows that site's real score in its grade colour.

Construction

  • 100-unit square. Stroke 7.5 units, round joins and caps.
  • The reading's end travels from 80 (score 0) to 16 (score 100).
  • Below 24 px, use the single W: one W, same reading stroke.
  • Clear space is the height of one W stroke on every side.

Never

  • Show a score the API didn't return.
  • Read the last stroke as a price. It is health, always.
  • Animate the reading except when a rating lands.
  • Add a leaf, a dollar sign or a price tag.

Proof of Life · the quarterly report

A seal with a date on it

Every quarter, WildWorth issues a Proof of Life for each site. The seal reuses the product's own Health instrument: a ring of 100 dots, one per point of health, lit up to the score. The centre carries the grade and the quarter. The proof is the reading plus the date.

On the machine layer
On paper
After a downgrade

How it reads

  • 100 dots: one per point of health, lit up to the score.
  • The letter: the grade, in the serif.
  • The stamp: the quarter it was issued, in mono.
  • A new seal every quarter. Old seals stay in the Record.

Never

  • Abbreviate it to “PoL”. It reads as crypto.
  • Use a heartbeat line. Every health app has one.
  • Show a newspaper or a date held up to camera.
  • Issue a seal without its evidence one click away.

Watch-outs

What could go wrong, and the rule that prevents it

“Worth” pulls toward money

The sandbox rules forbid anything that reads as a return to supporters.

Rule: worth is always what the land provides (water, carbon, flood protection). Never pair it with invest, returns or profit.

“Wild” can erase the people

Xochimilco's chinampas are farmed by its community, and a restoration forest is planted.

Rule: in copy, wild means “alive and able to renew itself”, never “untouched by people”. Stewards stay the protagonists.

Proof of Life's baggage

A kidnapping phrase with a crypto echo. Kept out of the company name for that reason.

Rule: always “Proof of Life”, in full, attached to a site and a quarter. Never PoL, never on its own as a slogan.

Clear space

Quick searches found no company called WildWorth, and no brand called Proof of Life in nature, satellites or conservation. Nearest: Wild, a UK deodorant brand.

Next: a trademark search (USPTO, EUIPO, WIPO, IP India) before anything public. Quick searches aren't legal clearance.
